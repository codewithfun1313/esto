Start with the evidence, not the asking price

The asking price is a marketing position; the evidence is what similar properties on similar streets actually achieved in the last six months. Before any conversation about offers, we pull verified comparables, adjust them for condition, floor area and outlook, and show you the full working. It is the difference between negotiating with an opinion and negotiating with a case.

Every number we put in front of a client is one we can trace back to a completed transaction.

Understand the cost of the things you cannot see

Roof condition, drainage falls, damp history, boundary responsibility, service charge trajectory and planned major works are the items that turn a good purchase into an expensive one. A survey answers some of these; the seller's document pack and a careful conversation with the managing agent answer the rest. Ask for both before you commit, not after your offer is accepted.

Think in total cost, not headline price

Stamp duty, legal fees, survey costs, immediate works and the first year of running costs routinely add eight to twelve percent to a purchase. Buyers who budget for that figure from the outset negotiate more calmly and complete more reliably, because nothing forces a late renegotiation.

Give yourself a decision rule

Write down, before you view anything, the three things the property must have and the one thing you will never accept. It sounds mechanical, and it is the single most effective protection against a persuasive viewing on a sunny afternoon.
